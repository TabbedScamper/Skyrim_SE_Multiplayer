## Mechanism and files

Death and knockdown hooks seed a priority queue, with bounded discovery fallback. Follower interpolates complete sets, extrapolates at most 100 ms, and applies COM-aware velocity steering before the native solver. It retains dynamic inertia subtypes, preserves engine-native keyframed/fixed helpers, and never assigns keyframed motion. Position correction is bounded to 10 game units per step with a 40 ms response time; velocity feed-forward avoids adding that full lag to owner motion. Constraints are unchanged.
- Settling requires owner rest (1.5 seconds below 2.1 units/s and 0.05 rad/s), compatible follower motion, and a 500 ms follower dwell under the SAME speed thresholds. Nonfinite velocities cannot count as rest. Full-set exact placement happens at the post-solver boundary; each setter rechecks active authority, world/UID and mutation state because native 60918 can drain callback-enqueued removal. An interrupted set is not reported as exact. Islands are requested to sleep only when every member belongs to this streamed set and is resting; unrelated contacts prohibit forced sleep even when slow. The next observation measures persistence independently. Fixed helpers must already match. A later impact resumes steering. The tighter gate may delay or prevent the fallback in continuing contacts; that is preferable to labeling moving contacts settled, but convergence remains unmeasured.

## SCALE bounds and limits

Let P be interested players, A_total the total distinct active ragdolls across owners, and B bodies per ragdoll (B <= 64). Capture across owners is O(A_total B), relay is O((P-1) A_total B). With A ragdolls per owner and P simultaneous owners, A_total=P*A, so system-wide fan-out is O(P squared A B). The previous unconditional exclusion of quadratic growth was wrong. A host-owned set shared by P followers does not multiply capture work by P. Each body carries 53 bytes before message overhead. At the nominal 33 ms period, one 18-body corpse contributes about 28.9 KB/s upstream, 115.6 KB/s to four followers, or 260.2 KB/s to nine, multiplied by active corpse count. These are payload estimates, not measurements or capacity claims.

Per 33 ms refresh: eight packed local candidates, at most 32 capture actors (body and optional head), and 64 remote bindings. Dismember retries visit at most 16 entries per frame; retained binding retirement is limited to 64 per main frame. Death/knockdown hints bypass discovery. At saturation, capture revisit is roughly `ceil(A_owner/32)*33 ms`, publication `ceil(streams/64)*33 ms`; neither is a guaranteed 30 Hz. Ordinary discovery latency is `ceil(localActors/8)*33 ms`. Native work selects at most 64 streams times 64 bodies per world step, round-robin; more streams receive fewer steering steps. All bodies of a selected ragdoll are processed together. Exceptional sleep checks visit at most 128 members per distinct island, each checked against at most B owned bodies, O(I*128*B), I <= B <= 64, only on a settle attempt. Immutable stream snapshots allocate on publication. Mutable StepBinding fields rely on the native serial step for the same world and wrapper locking; shared_ptr alone does not prove thread safety. No new per-step heap allocation is introduced by this revision. Existing world-queue pop/push and shared ownership can have allocator/refcount costs, so the earlier blanket zero-allocation/performance claim is withdrawn pending profiling.

Harness rendering visits one scheduled actor and at most 128 bone nodes, with names limited to 128 bytes; no actor or scene-tree census is added. Five- and ten-player CPU, memory and transport results remain unmeasured. Profile only after paired correctness, as required by AGENTS.md.
